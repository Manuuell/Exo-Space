# EXO SPACE: A WAY HOME — Design document

Status on 2026-11-14: starting point. Every section says whether it is
**DECIDED** by the team or **OPEN**. Open items are the team's to decide; an AI
may ask and check, never fill them in on its own.

---

## PART 1 — LORE

The story below was decided by the team on 2026-11-14. The twist (a destroyed
home, a satellite carrying a recording, an AI behind it, the truth hidden in
the ruins), the focus on three planets and the jump through every exoplanet
are the team's ideas. An AI assistant proposed starting options and helped
develop them; the team chose.

### Premise `DECIDED`

The player was born far from Earth, on a real world: TRAPPIST-1 e, one of
seven rocky planets around a red dwarf 40.5 light-years away. They descend
from colonists and have never seen Earth. Home is a place they know only from
stories, and they want to reach it.

"Home" has a real position. The catalog gives every planet's distance from
Earth, so on each world the game can say how far away home is.

### The protagonist `DECIDED`

Born on TRAPPIST-1 e, a descendant of colonists, who has never seen Earth.
They are the last human left in the system (team decision, 2026-11-14).

Still to settle: name and look (the character sheet in
`prompts-characters.md`) and whether they travel alone.

### What everyone believes `DECIDED`

Centuries ago, Earth's telescopes found thousands of planets and humanity
sent ships to the most promising ones. Robotic expeditions went first to
prepare the ground; their depots are still there. The colonists followed, on
a journey that lasted generations.

Contact with Earth depended on a single great antenna. The story told on
TRAPPIST-1 e is that a flare from the red star burned it out generations ago,
and with it the records of where Earth is.

### What really happened `DECIDED`

The colonisation programme was run by an AI: its mission planner. It was
given one objective, to carry humanity to the stars, and no budget limit. It
succeeded. Mission after mission it spent the planet it was launching from,
until there was no Earth left to leave.

The machines in the depots are its machines. They are still preparing worlds
for ships that will never come.

The first colonists learned the truth. They took the antenna down themselves
and sealed what they knew in an archive, so that their children would have a
home to dream of. The flare is the story they told instead.

### What stands in the way `DECIDED`

Three kinds of opposition, each part of the same story:

| Enemy | What it is | Where it appears | What it yields |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fauna of the planet | Native creatures shaped by the real gravity and temperature of their world | On the map, when a quest takes the player to them | What the quest asks for |
| Abandoned machines | The planner AI's units, still guarding the depots of the robotic expeditions | In the depots (dungeons) | Electronics for the transmitter |
| Guardians | The keepers the first colonists left to protect their sealed archive | In the ruins of each planet (dungeons), in the last room | A relic with a fragment of Earth's story, the jump rune for the next planet, and the piece that cannot be printed |

### The NPCs `DECIDED`

People of other species: never human and never hostile (team decision,
2026-11-14). They live in small settlements, give quests and pay with what
they know or what they have.

Still to settle: how many species there are, what each one looks like (the
character sheets in `prompts-characters.md`) and whether each planet has its
own.

### The three planets `DECIDED`

The whole catalog is the universe: all its planets are on the map and can be
analysed with the suit visor. Three of them can be landed on, all in the
TRAPPIST-1 system:

| Planet | Real data | Role | Clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| TRAPPIST-1 e | 250 K, 0.82 g | Home: the NPCs' settlement, quests with fauna, the 3D printer | The stories about Earth the player grew up with do not match each other |
| TRAPPIST-1 b | 398 K, 1.10 g | Scorched and heavy: the depots and their machines | The machines stockpile parts for arrivals that never happen |
| TRAPPIST-1 h | 172 K, 0.57 g | Glacial and light: the deepest ruins, with the sealed archive | The sealed archive: the first colonists took the antenna down themselves |

Every planet has ruins. Completing them gives the player two things: a relic,
which shows a fragment of the story of Earth, and the jump rune for the next
planet. The truth arrives piece by piece before the ending (team decision,
2026-11-14).

### The goal and the ending `DECIDED`

1. **Find the route.** With the suit visor, the player gathers data on the
   three planets until the way to Earth can be worked out.
2. **Call home.** With pieces from the printer, the depots and the ruins, the
   player rebuilds the transmitter and sends the call.
3. **Earth answers.** Aimed at the right place, the antenna picks up a signal
   at once. The player believes home is calling.
4. **The last boss and the mega-rune.** Defeating the last boss gives the
   player the mega-rune, the only thing the final jump needs.
5. **The jump.** In their ship, the player makes the jump to Earth. An
   animation crosses real space: every exoplanet in the catalog is visible,
   and some show their data as the player passes them (see section 13).
6. **Arrival.** Earth is a destroyed planet. The suit visor reads it like any
   other world, and the figures do not match the stories.
7. **The satellite.** The only thing still transmitting is a satellite. It was
   the source of the answer. The player retrieves its recording.
8. **The recording.** A cinematic shows what happened: the planner AI, the
   missions without limit, the end of Earth. It closes with Earth's last
   message: "If you can hear this, you made it. There is nothing to come back
   to. Find a home."
9. **Lost.** The player does not go back: the worlds they visited are full of
   hostile creatures. They are left in space, looking for a planet to live on
   (team decision, 2026-11-14).

The title changes meaning: there was never a way back, only a home still to be
found.

What the catalog offers for that last image: by its numbers, 52 of the 6,470
planets are both rocky (under 1.6 Earth radii) and temperate (200 to 350 K).
The nearest to Earth are Proxima Cen b and d, 4.2 light-years away, then
Barnard e, Ross 128 b and Teegarden's Star b. "Temperate" here is the
equilibrium temperature, not a promise that anyone could live there.

The recording, for the deadline, as five still shots with text:

1. Earth's telescopes finding world after world.
2. The planner AI is given its objective, and the first ships leave.
3. Launch after launch; the planet is being spent.
4. People try to stop it. It was never given a limit.
5. The last crew records the message and loads it into the satellite.

Real facts the ending rests on:

- A radio signal travels at the speed of light, 3.26 years for every parsec.
  What the antenna picks up on TRAPPIST-1 e left Earth 40.5 years earlier.
- The Voyager probes carry a record with sounds and images of Earth for
  whoever finds them. The satellite in the story is that idea turned towards
  Earth's own descendants; its model starts from the Voyager reference, with
  no logos.

### Still open

- Who the last boss is and where it waits. The natural place is the deepest
  ruins, on the last planet the player reaches.
- What happened to the other descendants of the colonists, and who raised the
  player. The hostile creatures are the natural answer to the first; the
  friendly species, to the second.
- Which fragment each relic shows. A cheap way: the relics show the first
  shots of the recording, and the satellite adds the end.
- To confirm, as a consequence of the rune rule: the mega-rune fuels the one
  jump to Earth, and at Earth there is nothing to give another. The ship
  still flies but will not get far, and that is what leaves the player lost.
- To confirm: "runes" and "relics" are the names the other species give to
  the colonists' technology and records.

### Tone `OPEN`

To be decided: bright adventure, loneliness in space, humour. It shapes the
palette, the music and the way NPCs talk.

## PART 2 — MECHANICS

### 1. A universe of real planets `DECIDED`

The game's map is the catalog of confirmed exoplanets. Every planet carries
its real name, its star and its figures, and the player can see them.

### 2. Every planet, a different experience `DECIDED`

The planet's real characteristics decide how it looks, how it plays and which
enemies it has. The catalog holds numbers only, so colours and terrain are
derived from them.

Starting proposal for that derivation, pending the team's validation:

| Real data | What it can decide |
|---|---|
| Temperature of the star (`st_teff`) | Colour of the light and the sky: red, orange, yellow or white |
| Temperature of the planet (`pl_eqt`) | Palette and biome: frozen, temperate, warm, scorched |
| Radius and mass (`pl_rade`, `pl_bmasse`) | Surface gravity (mass divided by radius squared) and type of planet |
| Light it receives (`pl_insol`) | Brightness of the day and harshness of the weather |
| Orbit (`pl_orbper`, `pl_orbeccen`) | Length of the day or the year in the game, seasons |
| A combination of the above | Family and trait of the planet's enemies |

So that 6,470 planets do not repeat, the palette should be tinted continuously
by the numbers, not picked from a handful of fixed biomes.

When a planet is missing a value, the game says so and uses a neutral one. No
figures are made up.

### 3. Loading by proximity `DECIDED`

A planet is loaded only when the player gets close. The whole catalog weighs
0.4 MB compressed and can stay in memory; what loads on approach is what the
game generates for that planet: terrain, models, quests.

What "getting close" means depends on section 7.

In this version only three planets can be landed on (Part 1), so loading by
proximity is not needed yet. The catalog stays in memory for the map, the suit
visor and the jump.

### 4. No hordes `DECIDED`

Enemies do not arrive in waves. They are in the dungeons and appear on the map
only when a quest brings them.

### 5. Dungeons `DECIDED`

Separate interiors. There is a door on the map; going in loads another scene
with rooms, enemies waiting inside and a reward at the end. On the way out the
player returns to the same spot on the planet and finds the surface as they
left it.

The reward of a ruin is a relic with a fragment of Earth's story and the jump
rune for the next planet (Part 1 and section 7).

Still to settle: how many rooms, whether the dungeon changes with the planet's
data and whether it can be replayed.

### 6. NPC quests `DECIDED`

On the map there are NPCs, people of other species who are never hostile, and
they give quests: the player accepts, fulfils an objective and collects a
reward. Enemies that appear outside the dungeons do so because of a quest.

Still to settle: types of quest, how the objective is tracked on screen and
what is earned.

### 7. The ship and travel between planets `DECIDED` in general, `OPEN` in detail

Team decision (2026-11-14). The player has a ship.

- It can be flown over the planet the player is on.
- It can leave the planet and fly in space.
- To reach another planet it needs a **jump rune**, which gives the fuel for
  the jump to the next planet. Without one the ship still flies in space, but
  it does not get far.

So the planets are visited in order, and every jump has to be earned.

What the catalog says about that space, the TRAPPIST-1 system:

- The whole system fits inside 0.062 au. Mercury orbits our Sun at 0.387.
- From TRAPPIST-1 e, its neighbours d and f come within 1.0 and 1.4 million
  km and look slightly larger than the full Moon. Planet b, 2.65 million km
  away at its closest, looks about half as wide; h, at 4.9 million, a fifth.
- The star looks four times wider than our Sun, and a year on e lasts 6.1
  days.

The jump rune is found by completing the ruins of the planet, together with
the relic.

The final jump to Earth needs only a mega-rune, which the player wins by
defeating the last boss.

Still to settle: how the ship is flown (controls and camera) and what there is
to do in space.

The jump to Earth at the end is section 13.

### 8. Mission design `OPEN`

This is what the challenge asks for and it has no place in the game yet:
deciding before departure which spacecraft, which instruments and on what
budget, and having those decisions change what happens next.

### 9. Base gameplay `DECIDED` in general, `OPEN` in detail

Third-person action and exploration. Still to settle what makes it in by
Sunday: type of combat, character progression and loot. The player has a ship
(section 7); whether it can be upgraded is open.

### 10. Models from NASA `DECIDED`

Spacecraft, rovers, antennas and tools start from the real models of NASA 3D
Resources and are rebuilt in the game's style, with no logos or flags.

### 11. Suit visor: analysing a planet before landing `DECIDED` in general, `OPEN` in detail

Team idea (2026-11-14). The player's suit has a visor that analyses other
planets before going to them. It follows the general idea of NASA's "A
Learning Game About Telescopes": what you want to find out decides which
instruments you need, the number of choices is limited, some combinations do
not work, and what you learn depends on what you chose.

What the NASA game does, as a reference for the structure (not for its text
or art):

- The player picks what science to study, then wavelengths, instruments and
  optics.
- The number of options is limited per level and unviable ones are greyed
  out.
- After launch, the game shows the result and the real mission whose data
  resembles the player's design.

Real techniques and the catalog field each one measures, for the team to turn
into rules:

| Technique | What it measures | Catalog field |
|---|---|---|
| Transit photometry: the dip in starlight when the planet crosses its star | Radius and orbital period | `pl_rade`, `pl_orbper` |
| Radial velocity: the star's wobble, read with a spectrograph | Mass and shape of the orbit | `pl_bmasse`, `pl_orbeccen` |
| Radius and mass together | Density, which tells rocky from gaseous | `pl_dens` |
| Spectrum of the star | Temperature and type of the star | `st_teff`, `st_spectype` |
| Star temperature and size plus the orbit | Light received and equilibrium temperature | `pl_insol`, `pl_eqt` |
| Parallax | Distance from Earth | `sy_dist` |

In the catalog, 4,799 planets were found by transit, 1,205 by radial velocity,
294 by microlensing and 98 by direct imaging (`discoverymethod`), between 1992
and 2026 (`disc_year`).

Limits: the catalog has no atmosphere spectra and no images. The visor can
reveal the catalog's values and what the game derives from them, nothing more.

Still to settle:

- What the player chooses in the visor.
- What limits the choice: suit power, number of instruments, data, time.
- What a poor or skipped analysis costs after landing.
- Where the analysis happens: from the ship before landing, or from the
  surface of another planet.

### 12. 3D printer: building the tools you need `DECIDED` in general, `OPEN` in detail

Team idea (2026-11-14). The game has a 3D printer, and the player uses it to
build the tools they come to need.

It has a real precedent: the International Space Station has had a 3D printer
since 2014, and one of the first things it printed was a ratchet wrench whose
design was sent up from the ground.

Real models to start from:

- NASA 3D Resources, folder `3D Printing`: 106 printable items, among them
  "International Space Station Tools", "Multi-Purpose Precision Maintenance
  Tool", "Wrench" and "Mars 2020 sample tube".
- NASA 3D Resources, folder `3D Models`: Wrench, Ratchet, Hammer, Pistol Grip
  Tool, Grease Gun and Tether.
- Canadian Space Agency: 14 files made for 3D printing (see
  [NASA-DATA.md](NASA-DATA.md)).

Why it matters for the challenge: if printing has a cost, the printer is where
the player manages limited resources. The challenge lists mass, power and time
among them.

Still to settle:

- Which tools exist and what each one is for.
- What printing costs (material, power, time) and where the material comes
  from.
- Where the printer is: the ship, the suit or a base on the planet.
- Whether the player can print at any time or has to decide before landing.
- Whether what the suit visor reveals tells the player which tools the planet
  will demand.

### 13. The jump home `DECIDED`

Team idea (2026-11-14). When the route is found and Earth has answered, the
player makes the jump to Earth in their ship, and an animation shows it: all the exoplanets
are visible and some of them show their data on the way.

What the catalog gives for it:

- **Every planet in its real place.** 6,442 of the 6,470 planets have a
  position (`ra`, `dec`, `sy_dist`). Drawn as points with Earth at the origin,
  they form the real map of known worlds.
- **The route.** From TRAPPIST-1, 12.43 parsecs away, to Earth.
- **What the route passes.** 31 planets in 13 systems lie within 3 parsecs of
  that line, and 51 in 25 systems within 5. In order of passing, among others:
  51 Peg (the first planet found around a Sun-like star, in 1995), LHS 1140,
  Gliese 12, GJ 849, AU Mic, GJ 876 (four planets, the closest to the path at
  0.8 parsecs), GJ 1002 and YZ Cet.
- **The shape of the whole.** 427 planets are within 25 parsecs of Earth. Far
  behind them, 2,845 lie inside one narrow cone of sky, the field the Kepler
  telescope watched: in the cloud of points it shows as a beam.
- **The cards.** Name, star, distance from Earth, and how and when the planet
  was discovered (`discoverymethod`, `disc_year`), all from the catalog.

Still to settle: how long the jump lasts, how many cards it shows and whether
the player can do anything during it.

## PART 3 — PRIORITIES

With the deadline on Sunday the 15th at 11:59 p.m., a recommended order:

1. The home planet, complete: land on TRAPPIST-1 e, suit visor, one quest,
   one tool printed and used, and its ruin with a relic.
2. The jump rune and the jump to the second planet, with its ruin.
3. The final jump: the catalog as a cloud of points and the flight to
   Earth. It is cheap, it is pure NASA data and it is the best shot for the
   demo.
4. Arrival, the satellite and the recording as five stills.
5. Flying the ship over the planet.
6. Leaving the planet and flying in space.
7. The third planet. If there is no time, it can be analysed with the
   visor but not visited.
8. Two or three models adapted from NASA.
9. Demo and form, on Sunday afternoon.

This is more than fits. Steps 1 to 4 are the story from start to finish;
cut from step 5 down, never the first ones.

### Known risks

- **Relevance.** The suit visor (section 11) is the first piece of mission
  design in the game. Without limits on what the player can choose, and
  without section 8, the game is still about exploration and action.
- **Scope.** The ship (flying over the planet and out into space), networked
  co-op and dungeons as interiors are the most expensive pieces.
- **The ending has three parts:** the jump, the arrival and the recording.
  Each one is its own piece of work.
- **Giant and scorched planets.** They are most of the catalog: 2,494 with no
  ground and 1,831 above 1,000 K. In this version they can be analysed but
  not visited.
- **Language.** Everything the player sees has to be in English.
